# The worksheet

Three shopper types, because people arrive at a Christmas gift page in three completely
different states of mind. One generic "Christmas" collection serves none of them well.

| Type | What they know | What they need from the page |
|---|---|---|
| **Family & Friends** | Who it is for. Not what to buy. | Ideas, sorted by recipient and interest |
| **Gotta Gift** | The budget, and that it is obligatory. Kris Kringle, a teacher, Secret Santa. | A price band, fast, with no agonising |
| **Last Minute** | That they have left it too late. | Whether it can possibly arrive in time, and what to do if not |

**Fill all three.** A store that only answers Family & Friends loses the Kris Kringle
shopper entirely, and that shopper is the easiest sale of the three: they have a budget,
a deadline, and no emotional attachment to getting it exactly right.

## Section 1 — Family & Friends

**Who are they buying for?** Wife, husband, partner, child, sibling, friend, plus anything
the store's own data suggests. Do not take all six: pick the ones this catalogue genuinely
serves. A store selling men's shaving gear has no "gifts for child" tile, and an empty
tile is worse than a missing one.

**What are they interested in?** The interest, not the product category. "Someone who
cooks" rather than "kitchenware". This is what the tile copy says and what the SEO section
targets, because it is what a person types into a search box.

**What products or collections suit?** Six slots. Pull real products from the catalogue
here rather than asking the member to recall them, then confirm.

## Section 2 — Gotta Gift

**How much are they prepared to spend?** Under $50, $50 to $100, $100 to $200, $200+.
**Check these bands against the actual catalogue before offering them.** A store whose
cheapest item is $80 should not have an "under $50" tile, and one whose range tops out at
$60 needs narrower bands. Offer bands that have real products in them.

**Why are they buying it?** Kris Kringle, Bad Santa, a donation, a teacher gift. These are
occasions with their own search volume and their own price expectations. A Bad Santa gift
and a teacher gift at the same price point are not the same product.

**What products or collections suit?** Price bands are the one place a smart collection
genuinely earns its place: a rule on variant price keeps the tile accurate as stock and
pricing change through December, with nobody maintaining it.

## Section 3 — Last Minute

**When do they need it?** Within a week, tomorrow, instantly. The honest answer changes
daily as the cut-off approaches, which is why the cut-off date is on the page rather than
assumed.

**What options can you give?** Express shipping, on-demand delivery, click and collect,
gift cards. **Only list what the store actually offers.** A pick-up tile on a store with no
physical location is the fastest way to lose someone's trust in December.

**Gift cards are the whole answer to this section** for most stores. Instant, never the
wrong size, and available at 11pm on 24 December. If the store sells them, they lead here.

## Section 4 — The page around the gifts

Three things the worksheet does not ask for but the page needs, so ask them here.

**The banner.** A campaign line and an image for the top. The heading is the page's H1, so
write it as the thing itself — "Christmas Gift Guide" plus the brand, not a slogan nobody
searches.

**The quick links.** Four to six tiles into the collections the gift groups point at, for
the many people who arrive already knowing who they are buying for. Label them the way a
shopper would say it out loud: "Gifts for latte lovers", not "Collection 3".

**The SEO paragraphs.** One per gift group, 150-250 words, saying who it suits and why and
naming the actual products. This is most of what the page can rank on and most of what an
answer engine can quote, and it is the part most often skipped because it comes last.
Write it for a person: the reader who finds it useful is the same signal the search engine
is looking for. Draft it from the worksheet answers and the product details rather than
asking the member to write it — then have them correct it.

## What to settle before building

- **The delivery cut-off date**, per shipping method, in the store's own timezone. This
  goes in a bar across the top of the page and it is the single most-read thing on it.
- **Whether there is a physical pick-up location**, and its address and hours.
- **Whether gift cards exist** and their URL.
- **The campaign line and banner image** for the top of the page.

Ask for the cut-off date explicitly and confirm it back with the weekday. "Orders by
Friday 19 December" is checkable; "orders by the 19th" invites someone to get the day
wrong.
