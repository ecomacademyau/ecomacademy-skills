# Making the guide findable

Two different jobs. **Search** needs the page to rank for what people type. **Answer
engines** need it to be the thing an AI quotes when someone asks ChatGPT what to buy their
dad. The second is newer and most gift guides do nothing for it at all.

## Search

### Target the recipient, not the season

"Christmas gift guide" is a term every store on earth competes for and nobody wins. The
volume is in the long tail, and the long tail is **who the gift is for**:

```
gifts for [recipient] australia          gifts for [recipient] under $50
last minute gifts for [recipient]        [interest] gifts for [recipient]
christmas gifts for someone who [does X]
```

The worksheet's "what are they interested in" answer feeds this directly. "Someone who
cooks" becomes a heading someone could plausibly search; "kitchenware" does not.

### The page itself

- **One H1**, the guide's name, with the year in it. Year-stamped pages get clicked over
  undated ones in December, and it tells a crawler the page is current.
- **H2 per section**, written as the search rather than the label. "The top 5 gifts for
  someone who cooks" beats "Family & Friends".
- **Real links, not just tiles.** The image tiles are for people; the text links in the
  SEO blocks are what gets followed and what passes value to the collections. Keep both.
- **Alt text that describes the gift**, not the layout. "Cast iron pan, a gift for someone
  who cooks" not "tile 1".
- **Link down to the collections, and link back up from them.** A gift guide with no
  inbound links from the rest of the store is an island.

### Give it time to be found

**A guide published in mid December will not rank for Christmas.** Google needs weeks.
Publish by early November, submit the URL for indexing the day it goes live, and expect
the traffic to arrive in the last fortnight. Say this to the member plainly if they are
building it late: paid and email will carry this year, and the page will be ready and aged
for next year.

**Keep the same URL every year.** `/pages/christmas-gift-guide`, not
`/pages/christmas-gift-guide-2026`. A URL that accrues links and history for five
Christmases is worth far more than a fresh one each November. Update the content, keep the
address.

## Answer engines

This is where the opportunity is, because almost nobody is doing it.

### Answer the question in a sentence a machine can lift

Somebody asks an assistant "when do I need to order from [brand] for Christmas". For the
page to be the source of that answer, the cut-off has to exist **as a plain sentence in
the text**, not only as a graphic in a bar:

> "Order by 11am on Friday 19 December 2026 for standard delivery before Christmas within
> Australia. Express orders close Tuesday 23 December."

Specific, dated, unhedged. That is quotable. "Order early to avoid disappointment" is not.

### The questions to answer on the page

Put these in a real FAQ section with real answers, because they are what gets asked:

- When is the last day to order for Christmas delivery?
- Do you offer express shipping, and what is the cut-off?
- Can I pick up in store, and when are you open before Christmas?
- Do you sell gift cards, and are they instant?
- What if it arrives late, or the gift is wrong? *(the returns window over Christmas is
  asked constantly and almost never answered on the page)*
- What are good gifts under $50?

**Extend the returns window over Christmas if the store can**, and say so. "Anything
bought from 1 November can be returned until 31 January" removes the single biggest
hesitation in gift buying, and it is a sentence an answer engine will quote.

### Structured data

Two blocks of JSON-LD, generated by the script, both describing what is actually on the
page:

- **`ItemList`** per gift section, with each product's name, URL, image and price. This is
  how a product list becomes something an engine can read as a list rather than as prose.
- **`FAQPage`** for the questions above, with the answers exactly as they appear in the
  visible text.

**Never put a claim in the schema that is not visible on the page.** It is against every
search engine's guidelines, and more practically, it is how a store ends up with a rich
result promising something the page does not say.

### Write for being quoted

- **Specific beats persuasive.** "Ships same day if ordered before 11am" is quotable.
  "Fast shipping" is noise.
- **One fact per sentence.** Compound sentences get truncated badly when extracted.
- **No hedging on anything checkable.** A date, a price, a cut-off: state it.
- **Put the answer first**, then the detail. An engine lifting the first sentence of a
  section should get the answer, not a wind-up.

## The check

- One H1, year included, same URL as last year
- Every H2 is a phrase someone would plausibly search
- The cut-off date appears as a plain sentence, not only in the banner
- FAQ answers in the visible text match the FAQPage schema exactly
- Every ItemList entry points at a real, in-stock, correctly priced product
- Nothing in the schema is absent from the page


## Built as sections: what changes

**The H1 is the banner heading.** One per page. If another section is also set as H1, turn
one off — two H1s is a signal that the page does not know what it is about.

**The see-all link is a ranking signal, not furniture.** "See all gifts for latte lovers"
tells a reader and a crawler what sits behind the link; "See all" tells neither anything.
Every gift group gets link text that names the thing, and `--check` fails the build if it
says only "See all" or "Shop now".

**The SEO paragraphs at the foot are the body copy.** A gift guide is mostly images and
product titles, which is very little text to rank on. A paragraph per group — who it
suits, why, named products — is what turns the page from a grid into something that can
rank and be quoted. Put it at the foot where it does not get in the way of someone who
just wants to shop.

**The ItemList comes from the grid itself.** Each gift group emits its own ItemList naming
its products in order, built from the same blocks that render the tiles, so it cannot
describe products the page does not show.

**The FAQ answers the questions people actually type.** "When is the last day to order for
Christmas delivery?" is a real search with real volume every December, and the store is the
only authority on its own answer. Answer in the first sentence and add detail after it —
an answer engine quotes the first sentence.
