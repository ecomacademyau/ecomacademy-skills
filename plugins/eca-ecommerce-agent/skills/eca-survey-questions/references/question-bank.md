# Question bank

Every question the skill can use, with wording, select type, when to use it and how to build the answers. Pick the wording variant that sounds most like the brand. Tailor every answer list to the brand's data. The example answers here only show the level of specificity to aim for.

---

## New customer survey

### Attribution: How did you hear about us?

- **Wording:** "How did you first hear about us?" (preferred, "first" pushes people past the last thing they clicked) or "How did you hear about us?"
- **Type:** single-select, randomised, "Other (please specify)" pinned last.
- **Use when:** default yes. Skip only for truly single-channel brands.
- **Why we ask:** platform attribution over-claims and misses word of mouth, AI recommendations and offline. This shows where demand really starts.
- **Building the answers:**
  - Split platforms people can name: Instagram, Facebook and TikTok are separate options. People can't tell a Meta placement from an organic post, but they know which app they were in.
  - Always include: "Friend or family recommendation", "Google search", "AI assistant (ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.)".
  - Add only channels the brand actually runs: "Influencer or creator", "YouTube", "Podcast", "Pinterest", "In a store", "At a market", "Saw it in the news or a magazine", "Email from a partner".
  - Stay within 7 options plus Other. If the brand runs more than that, merge the smallest channels.
  - Avoid "Social media" as one option. It hides the answer you need.

### Who are you buying for?

- **Wording:** "Who are you buying for today?"
- **Type:** single-select, randomised, Other pinned last.
- **Use when:** gifting brands, or reviews regularly mention buying for others, or gift wrap/notes are offered, or sales spike around gifting moments.
- **Why we ask:** a gift buyer and a self buyer need different ads, offers and landing pages. A high gift share also changes the calendar (Mother's Day, Christmas).
- **Building the answers:** "Myself" plus the recipients the reviews mention. Examples: "My partner", "My mum", "A friend", "My kids", "A colleague or client". Keep "Myself" in the list even for heavy gifting brands.

### Purchase motivation: Why did you buy from us?

- **Wording:** "What made you decide to buy from us?" or "What was the main reason you bought from us today?"
- **Type:** single-select (forces the main reason), randomised, Other pinned last.
- **Use when:** always. Core question.
- **Why we ask:** the most chosen answer is the brand's lead angle. The second and third are the next angles to test.
- **Building the answers:** mine 4 to 7 distinct reasons from the "why I bought" lines in reviews, brand DNA, PDP claims and the brand's winning ad angles. The customer hasn't received the product yet, so phrase every option as something they wanted or knew before buying, never as an experienced result. Mix the types of reason so the list doesn't become seven versions of the same benefit:
  - The promised result ("A tan that won't transfer onto my sheets", not "It doesn't transfer")
  - The ingredients or materials ("Clean, natural ingredients")
  - Social proof ("The reviews convinced me")
  - A recommendation ("A friend swore by it")
  - Values ("Australian made", "Small business")
  - The offer ("The bundle deal", "Free shipping")
  - The format or convenience ("Dries in minutes")

### Alternatives considered: What did you consider before buying from us?

- **Wording:** "What else did you consider before choosing us?"
- **Type:** multi-select, randomised. Pin "Nothing, I came straight to you" and Other to the bottom.
- **Use when:** competitive categories with real substitutes.
- **Why we ask:** shows what the brand is really up against, which sets the comparison angle for ads ("us vs the salon") and the "Us vs them" section on the PDP.
- **Building the answers:** solution types, never brand names. Include the do-nothing option where it is real. Examples for self-tan: "A salon spray tan", "Another self-tan brand", "A gradual tan moisturiser", "Tanning in the sun", "Going without".

### Objection: What almost stopped you from buying?

- **Wording:** "What almost stopped you from buying today?" or "Was there anything that nearly stopped you from ordering?"
- **Type:** single-select (required for branching), randomised. Pin "Nothing, it was an easy yes" and Other to the bottom.
- **Use when:** always. Core question.
- **Why we ask:** each objection is something to counter in ads, on the PDP, in the FAQ or in the checkout. Results show objections buyers overcame, not the ones that lost the sale.
- **Building the answers:** see `objection-followups.md`. Pick the 4 to 6 objections with the most evidence for this brand and word each one specifically.

### Objection follow-up (branched)

- **Wording:** one per objection, for example "What made the price feel like a hurdle?"
- **Type:** multi-select, randomised, Other pinned last. 3 to 6 options.
- **Shown:** only to people who chose that objection. "Nothing" and "Other" skip it.
- **Why we ask:** "price" on its own is not actionable. "Shipping added too much" and "Wasn't sure it was worth it" need completely different fixes.

### What problem are you trying to solve?

- **Wording:** "What were you hoping our product would help you with?" (softer) or "What problem are you trying to solve?"
- **Type:** open text, optional.
- **Use when:** problem-solving products.
- **Why we ask:** the strongest persona signal there is. It gives the customer's own words for the pain point, ready to use in hooks and headlines.

### What would you love to see from us? (new customers)

- **Wording:** "Is there anything you'd love to see from us next?"
- **Type:** open text, optional.
- **Use when:** only if a slot is left after the higher priorities. Returning customers usually answer it better.

## Returning customer survey

### What made you come back?

- **Wording:** "What brought you back today?"
- **Type:** single-select, randomised, Other pinned last.
- **Why we ask:** the repurchase trigger. It shows whether retention runs on results, replenishment, new products or discounts, which shapes the email and SMS flows.
- **Building the answers:** examples: "I ran out", "I loved the results", "I wanted to try another product", "There was a sale or offer", "Buying it as a gift", "An email or text reminded me". Tailor to the brand (for subscription brands add "Changing or adding to my subscription").

### Who are you buying for? (returning)

Same as the new customer version. Only include if it qualified.

### What do you love most about our products?

- **Wording:** "What do you love most about our products?"
- **Type:** open text.
- **Why we ask:** the experienced customer's lead benefit, in their own words. Compare it with new customers' purchase motivation: a gap between why people buy and why they stay is a marketing opportunity.

### What else would you like to see from us?

- **Wording:** "What else would you love us to make?" or "What else would you like to see from us?"
- **Type:** open text.
- **Why we ask:** new product and bundle ideas from people who already trust the brand.

### How can we make your experience even better?

- **Wording:** "How could we make your experience with us, or with our products, even better?"
- **Type:** open text.
- **Why we ask:** product improvements and customer experience fixes from people who know the product best.

## Questions we deliberately don't ask

- **Age, gender, income.** Meta and Shopify already give a proxy, and these questions lower completion. The persona signal comes from "who are you buying for" and "what problem are you solving".
- **NPS or star ratings.** The review app already does this.
- **"How likely are you to buy again?"** Stated intent is a weak predictor. Shopify repeat data is the real answer.
