# Insight layer

The charts show what customers said. This layer turns it into things to do. Every item names its source in the data (a question and its count, a follow-up, or a quote). If it cannot, cut it.

Voice: direct, plain, "you" language, numbers for credibility. No hype words, no emoji, no em dashes. The brand's market spelling.

## Key insights (6 to 8)

Each card has:
- **title:** the finding in one line ("Three in ten nearly bought elsewhere because they couldn't smell it").
- **body:** two or three sentences with the number, its base and what it means.
- **action:** the one thing to do about it.
- **source**, and the **signal** of the number it rests on (copy the calculated tag from the built report if you need it in the text; never upgrade it).

Cover the range: the top objection, the top reason to buy, the gap between why people buy and why they come back, attribution, a persona or product-line insight, and anything surprising in the written answers. At least one card can be "what is not a problem" when the data says so (for example, price flat and low means no discount needed).

## Angles (from the motivation question)

One per motivation with 5+ all-time answers, ordered by share:
- **angle:** a short name ("Scent you can picture").
- **why:** one sentence on what buyers are telling you.
- **hooks:** 2 to 3 opening lines under 12 words, written in customer language. Mix structures: a statement, a question, a "you" line, a specific detail.
- **best_for:** which persona or product line it suits.
- **source.**

## Objection cards

One per objection with 5+ all-time answers, ordered by all-time share. Smaller objections go in one line under the cards.
- **summary:** what is really going on, in one or two sentences, drawing on the follow-up answers and written answers.
- **why:** the follow-up question results (raw counts).
- **quotes:** 2 to 3 verbatims.
- **counters:** answer the specific follow-up reason, not the headline objection.
  - **website:** what to change, on which page, in one concrete sentence (PDP, FAQ, cart, shipping page).
  - **ads:** 2 to 3 hooks that answer the objection.
  - **content:** one content idea (UGC brief, reel, comparison post, demo, founder video).
  - **email:** one line for the welcome or abandoned-cart flow.
- **source.**

## Attribution note

Two or three sentences comparing what customers say with how ad platforms report it, where you know the brand runs those platforms. Word of mouth and AI assistants are usually under-credited by platforms; say so when the data shows it.

## Personas (2 to 4)

Built only from survey data (who they buy for, the problem they are solving, motivation, product line). Each has:
- **name:** descriptive, not a made-up first name ("The scent-first self-buyer").
- **share:** roughly how much of the survey they represent, with the basis ("about a third: buying for themselves and chose scent").
- **who, wants, worries:** one line each.
- **angle:** the angle and hook that fits them.
- **source.**

## Retention moves

From the returning survey: 2 to 4 moves, each with the flow or touchpoint (replenishment, post-purchase, win-back, loyalty, gifting), what to do and the source.

## Action plan

Grouped into:
- **This week:** maximum 3. Quick, high-impact, low-risk (copy changes, a new ad hook test, an FAQ answer).
- **This month:** bigger builds (a PDP section, a new flow, a content series).
- **Test next:** ideas worth testing that rest on Early signals or written answers.

Each item: **channel** (Website, Ads, Content, Email, Product), **title**, **body** (what to do, concretely), **source**. Lead with the top objection's counter.

## Language bank

4 to 6 themes, each with 3 to 6 short customer phrases worth reusing in ads and on site, quoted as written (trim, do not correct). No names or identifying details.

## Questions to consider (optional)

If written answers keep raising a reason that has no answer option, suggest adding it. Add a note: changing the live survey resets all-time for that question, so batch changes and do them rarely.
