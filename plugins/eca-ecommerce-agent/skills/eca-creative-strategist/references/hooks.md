# Hooks (step 5)

The hook is the highest-leverage line in the ad and the one AI gets most wrong. Corny hooks come from writing for "an audience". Good hooks come from writing for one person you can picture, in one moment you can name, using words a real customer already used.

Hooks are chosen before the body is written. The hook makes a promise to a specific person; the body exists to pay it off.

## 1. Three layers that agree

| Layer | What it is | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Spoken | The first sentence | About 12 words max. Mid-thought, like the middle of a conversation |
| Visual | Frame one | Makes sense with the sound off. Something happening, not a face waiting to talk |
| On-screen text | A few words, top third | Names the person or the problem. Not a repeat of the spoken line word for word |

Most people scroll with the sound off. If the visual and the text don't stop them, the spoken line never gets heard.

## 2. Structural types

Use different types on the same angle. Meta's delivery rewards structurally different openers; five versions of the same type compete with each other.

| Type | Pattern | Trigger it pulls |
|---|---|---|
| Situation callout | "If your toddler's up at 5am every single day..." | Self-recognition: "that's me" |
| Problem first | "I've ruined three sets of lash extensions sleeping in a mask." | Shared pain |
| Outcome first | "Slept until 2pm after a night shift, first time in months." (only if true and allowed) | Desire, with specificity as proof |
| Proof or result first | Shows the result in frame one, then "okay so this is what happened" | Seeing is believing |
| Counter-conventional | "Everyone says buy a bigger harness. That's why your dog pulls." | Status-quo threat, curiosity |
| Confession | "I'm embarrassed how long I put up with this." | Vulnerability, trust |
| Mechanism reveal | "The reason every mask leaks light is right here." (points to the nose) | Curiosity gap |
| Social proof first (real only) | "My sister asked where I got this before I'd sat down." | Others like me chose it |
| Comparison | Holds up the old thing next to the new one | Contrast |
| Story in motion | "So I'm halfway through the school run and..." | Open loop |
| Pattern interrupt | Bins the old product in frame one | Surprise |
| Founder | "I started making these after [real moment]." | Origin, authenticity |

## 3. The mix per angle

Ship 3 to 5 hooks per angle, from different types, and always include:
- one **situation callout** (names who it's for, so the right people stop and the wrong ones don't),
- one **problem first**,
- one **proof or result first** (or a demo-first visual).

Add a spread of risk: a safe one, a medium one, and one that's slightly uncomfortable to say (a confession, a contrarian truth). The uncomfortable one often wins because nobody else would run it.

Each hook is its own full take on the **same body**, said word for word. That's how the member learns which opener works: one variable changes, everything else stays the same. Talking points can be improvised; the hook can't.

## 4. How to write them

1. Fill in, from the strategy and the evidence: the person, the moment, the pain or desire in their own words, the mechanism, the proof, the brand voice, the claims you can't make.
2. Draft at least 10 for the angle. Fast. Steal words from the customer quotes.
3. Run each through the tests below. Kill anything that fails one.
4. Pick 3 to 5 that cover the mix. For each, write the visual and the on-screen text.
5. Note the type and the trigger, so the member learns what they're testing.

## 5. The tests (kill on any fail)

- **Dinner table test**: would a real person say this sentence out loud to a friend? If it sounds like an ad, it is one.
- **Swap test**: put a competitor's name in. Still works? Too generic.
- **Specific noun test**: is there a specific person, object, place, time or number in it? "Still on after a 12-hour shift" passes; "long-lasting comfort" fails.
- **Promise test**: does the body pay off exactly what the hook promised? A hook that promises a story and delivers a feature list loses the viewer at second 4.
- **Truth test**: is every fact in it in the brand file, the reviews, the data, or confirmed by the member? Numbers and timeframes especially.
- **Policy test**: does it name a situation rather than assert a personal trait? (`compliance.md`) For body or health products, read the hook as if Meta's reviewer were the viewer: does it say or hint that *they* have the condition? "If you're the one minding the bags because it's that week" hints that the viewer has their period, so it fails. "I used to skip beach days every month" is the speaker's own story, so it passes.

## 6. Never

- Open with "Have you ever", "Hi, I'm", "Hey guys", "POV:", "Introducing", "Attention", "Stop scrolling", "You won't believe", "Here's why", "Let me tell you", "Did you know".
- Open with the brand name, a logo, a price or a discount.
- Stack rhetorical questions ("Tired of X? Sick of Y?").
- Use made-up stats, customer counts or timeframes.
- Use slop words: game-changer, elevate, unlock, level up, seamless, revolutionary, must-have, holy grail, obsessed (unless a customer said it).

## 7. Output (per angle)

| # | Spoken (word for word) | Visual (frame one) | On-screen text | Type | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | "If you've got lash extensions, don't sleep in a normal mask." | Close-up of squashed lashes in the mirror | lash extensions + sleep masks | Situation callout | safe |
| H2 | "I've ruined three sets of lashes sleeping in a mask." | Holds up an old flat mask, then bins it | $360 of lashes, gone | Problem first, confession | medium |
| H3 | (silent first second) lies down, mask on, eyes blink freely inside it | Eyes blinking inside the cups | blinking. inside the mask. | Proof first | spicy |

## 8. Iterating a winner

Keep the winning body exactly. Change only the opener, and change its **type**, not just its wording: if the winner opens on a problem, test a situation callout, a proof-first visual and a confession.
