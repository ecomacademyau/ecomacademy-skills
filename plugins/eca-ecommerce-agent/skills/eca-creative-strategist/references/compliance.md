# Compliance (check before every delivery)

Not legal advice. When a claim is borderline, flag it to the member and suggest they check. A rejected ad costs days; a misleading claim can cost far more.

## Meta advertising standards

- **Personal attributes.** Ads can't assert or imply the viewer's race, ethnicity, religion, age, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health (including medical conditions), financial status, criminal record or name. Write "for skin that gets dry in winter", not "your dry skin" or "Do you have acne?". "Over 40?" asserts age. "Struggling with anxiety?" asserts a health condition. Callout hooks name a **situation**, never a trait of the viewer. This includes implied and euphemistic versions: "because it's that week", "that time of the month", "POV: you're on day 2", "if you're dealing with thinning", "for your sensitive tummy". For products tied to the body or health, the safe patterns are first person ("I used to...", "my skin..."), third person about someone in the video ("she's wearing period swimwear right now"), or the moment ("beach day", "the 3pm slump"). Run the compliance check on every hook, on-screen text line and caption, not just the spoken script.
- **Health and wellness.** No content that makes people feel bad about their body, appearance or hygiene. No body-shaming close-ups. Weight-loss, supplement and cosmetic procedure ads must target 18+.
- **Before/after.** Only real, unfiltered footage, same light and angle, a stated timeframe, approved by the member. Avoid it entirely for weight loss and for anything implying a health outcome.
- **Ad matches landing page** on price, offer and product.

## TikTok (if the member runs it there too)

Stricter: no exaggerated or guaranteed results, no absolute claims ("number 1 on TikTok"), no misleading before/after, heavily AI-edited content labelled.

## Urgency and offers

- **No fake urgency.** "Ends tonight", "only this week", "selling out", "last chance", "limited time" only if literally true, with a real end date the member confirmed.
- No made-up "was" prices or discounts.
- The offer wording in the ad matches the landing page exactly.

## Claims (Australia; check local rules elsewhere)

- **Australian Consumer Law (ACCC).** Every claim, including inside a testimonial, must be true and backed by reasonable grounds: price, images, performance, delivery times. Intent doesn't matter.
- **"Number 1", "best", "most popular", "Australia's favourite".** Only with evidence the member can produce (sales data against the category, an independent award they can name). Otherwise cut it or swap in something provable ("over 2,000 five-star reviews" if that's true).
- **Refunds.** "No refunds" is unlawful wording; "no refunds for change of mind" is fine. Default: leave returns out of scripts unless the member gave approved wording.
- **Guarantees.** Only mention one the brand actually offers, in its real terms.
- **Testimonials.** Reviews must be genuine. Paid or gifted content is disclosed (labelled partnership ads). Don't edit out the negative parts to change the meaning. Results must be typical or qualified.
- **Cosmetics.** May claim to change appearance, cleanse, protect ("helps skin look smoother"). Physiological claims ("repairs skin", "stimulates collagen", "treats eczema", "stops hair loss", "regrows hair") make it a therapeutic good regulated by the TGA.
- **Food, drink and supplements.** Health claims ("supports digestion", "reduces bloating", "boosts immunity") only if they meet FSANZ Standard 1.2.7 (general level claims need a pre-approved food-health relationship and the right wording). Claims that a food treats, cures or prevents a disease ("cures bloating", "fixes IBS") are prohibited for foods. Listed medicines and supplements regulated by the TGA can only use permitted indications. Default to taste, ritual, routine and moment.
- **Baby and kids.** No safety claims beyond the product's actual certification (e.g. don't say "safe sleep" or mention SIDS unless the member supplies the certified wording). Follow the brand's approved safe-use wording. **Filming children:** no nappy area or bare skin on camera (frame a nappy change on hands, the zip and the product); any sleeping shot shows a clear cot and a child on their back, nothing loose in it; film "night" scenes in the day with the blinds down rather than waking a child; written consent from the parent; no child's name or location on screen. Meta also restricts ads that show children in some contexts, so keep it wholesome and obvious.
- **Pets.** No health or behavioural treatment claims ("cures anxiety") without substantiation.
- **Protected words.** Some words are legally protected (Olympic terms in Australia, for example). Don't use them unless licensed.
- **US**: the FTC endorsement guides (disclose paid talent; results typical or qualified). **UK**: the ASA/CAP code. Other markets: say the member should check.

## IP and people

- No competitor names, logos, footage or music. No celebrity names or likeness without a licence.
- No real customer names, faces or reviews without permission.
- Creators speak from their own real experience. Briefs say "only say what's true for you".

## When the member asks for a claim that breaks these

Say plainly which rule it breaks and why it matters in one or two lines (rejection, ACCC or TGA risk), then offer the strongest compliant version. Don't lecture, and don't silently soften it: the member needs to know the original line was the problem. Example: "'Cured my bloating in 7 days' is a disease claim for a food, which isn't allowed. What we can say, if it's true for her: 'It's the first greens powder I don't have to skull. Pineapple and mint, and I actually look forward to it now.' That sells the habit and the taste. Avoid softer body outcomes too ('feel lighter', 'less puffy', 'more energy'): they imply the same health result."

## Quick check (tick before delivery)

- [ ] No health, medical or therapeutic claims; no guaranteed results
- [ ] Before/after only under the rules above
- [ ] No "number 1", "best" or similar without evidence on file
- [ ] No fake urgency; offer matches the landing page
- [ ] No refund, returns or guarantee wording unless approved
