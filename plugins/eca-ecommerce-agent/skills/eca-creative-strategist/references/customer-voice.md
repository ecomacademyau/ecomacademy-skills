# Customer voice (step 2)

The best hooks and angles are almost always sitting in the member's reviews and survey answers, said better than any copywriter would say them. This step finds them and counts them.

## 1. Sources, best first

1. **Post-purchase survey answers** (or an eca-survey-report): the only source that tells you *why* someone bought and what nearly stopped them, unprompted.
2. **Reviews**: a CSV export beats scraping. Split verified reviews from imported or seeded ones first. Read the 1 to 3 star ones closely: that's where the objections live.
3. **Ad comments and DMs**: the questions real prospects ask out loud. Gold for comment replies.
4. **Support emails**: what goes wrong after purchase, which tells you what to set expectations on.
5. **Category reviews** (competitors' and marketplace reviews): their 1 to 3 star reviews show what the category gets wrong, which is often your best angle.

## 2. What to pull, with counts

| Pile | What you're looking for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Reasons to buy | Repeated words and outcomes, in their language | "31 of 212 reviews mention it doesn't squash lash extensions" |
| Who for | Self, partner, kid, gift, a specific job or life stage | "22 survey answers: bought for a partner" |
| The moment | When they decided, what had just happened | "after another night of the partner's phone light" |
| What it replaced | The old way or competitor they fired | "11 reviews compare it to a cheap airline mask" |
| Objections | What nearly stopped them, and what went wrong | "9 of 1 to 3 star reviews: strap too tight" |
| Unanswered questions | What prospects ask that no ad answers | "Does it work for side sleepers?" asked 14 times in comments |
| Surprise | Something people love that the brand never talks about | "6 reviews: they use it on planes" |

Record counts and sample size, not vibes. "Customers love it" is not evidence. Under 5 mentions is a lead, not a pattern; say so.

## 3. Grade what you find

- **A**: shows up in both ad results and customers' words.
- **B**: shows up in reviews or survey answers repeatedly.
- **C**: the member's hunch, or one or two mentions.

Angles built on A and B evidence go first. A C-grade angle can still be tested, but call it a hunch on the strategy page.

## 4. Friction and unanswered questions

After the account gaps, look for the friction. List the top questions and doubts a prospect has before buying, and check whether any live ad answers them. Each unanswered one is a candidate for a comment reply, a demo, or the proof beat of a yapper.

## 5. Keep the words

Save 10 to 20 short verbatim quotes (no names, no initials if they identify someone) with their source. Hooks and talking points should borrow these words directly. "My lashes don't get squashed" from a real customer beats "contoured comfort design" every time.

## 6. Privacy

Counts and short anonymised quotes only. No customer names, emails, order numbers or photos in any file. A real customer only appears in an ad with their permission.
