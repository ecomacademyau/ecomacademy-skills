# Founder story

A founder monologue, shot on a phone, that builds trust by telling the true story of the moment they realised the customer's problem was real, and what they did about it. This is the quality bar for every other format.

**Funnel:** TOF and MOF. **Length:** about 60 seconds (45 to 75). **Who:** the founder. Nobody else.

## Why it works

People keep watching because it feels like a real person telling the truth, not a brand selling. The viewer thinks "that's me" (you're describing my life), then "I need that" (your product is the way out). Start with "that's me" to earn trust, then earn "I need that". Keep one honest line alive to the end so it never tips into a pitch.

## Prerequisite: a real founder story

1. Look in `brand-data.md` §1 (founder story) and §3 (voice).
2. If it's missing or TBD, ask the member for what only they know: the founder's name and one-line personality, what personally frustrated them (or what they fell for), the moment it clicked, and what they made. A few questions is enough, and the rough, real answer is the good one.
3. **Never invent a founder, a backstory or a "moment".** If it didn't happen, it can't be in the script. That's the one thing that kills a founder ad.
4. No founder story to tell (faceless brand, founder won't go on camera, reseller with no origin)? This is the wrong format. Pick a yapper, demo or comment reply, which anyone can front.

## Format rule: talking to camera, one continuous take

The founder talks straight to camera in one take, like a Story on their own feed. No b-roll, no cutaways, no second angle. The power is unbroken eye contact; the moment you cut away it reads as an ad. The only thing on screen is the founder's face, and in beat 4 the product lifted into the same shot. Captions are fine.

## Step 1: the hook

Use `hooks.md` with these founder rules: a personal, raw, slightly vulnerable line. Lead with a confession, a contrarian truth or a small embarrassment. Specific enough to stop the scroll, universal enough that a thousand people feel "that's me". Never "Hi, I'm". No price. Ship 3: safe, medium, and one that makes the founder slightly uncomfortable.

## Step 2: pick the track

- **Track A: "My product fixes something that frustrated me."** The annoying problem, the thing that didn't work, the wasted money.
- **Track B: "My product gives a feeling people are missing."** The taste, the calm, the way it makes them feel, the thing nothing else delivered.

## Step 3: the six beats

Work one beat at a time. The founder answers each out loud first; write down what they actually said. Don't tidy it up: the rough version is the good version. The product isn't named until beat 4.

| Beat | Name | ~Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The hook | 3s | One of the 3 hooks. Said flat. No smile, no warm-up |
| 2 | Prove you lived it | 5s | The one specific detail that proves it was really their life: a real moment, real numbers. (A: the messy detail showing the problem was real. B: the moment everything out there felt cheap or fake.) Most founder ads die here |
| 3 | What you worked out | 10s | The penny-drop: usually, it was never the customer's fault, the right thing just didn't exist |
| 4 | What you made | 15s | Lift the product into frame, same shot. Plain words on what it does and how simple it is. No feature list |
| 5 | What changed | 15s | What's different now: the feeling, not the stat; the moment they knew it worked |
| 6 | The ask | 10s | Talk to one person. Name their exact situation, then tell them to try it. Like a friend. Brand and offer here |

The rhythm: that's me, that's me, what I worked out, here's what I made, here's what changed, here's the ask.

## Step 4: stitch and check

Put the six lines in order. Don't add joining words or smooth it. Read it aloud at normal pace: it should land near 60 seconds. Running over? Cut beat 4 to the steps (founders over-explain the product). Sounds like a brand wrote it? Have the founder say the line again like they're talking to a friend, and keep that version.

## Worked example (Track A, a desk lunch-warmer; shows the shape, don't copy)

- **Hook:** "I ate a cold lunch at my desk every day for two years and just accepted it."
- **Prove it:** "The microwave queue downstairs was ten people deep at noon, so I stopped bothering. Sad sandwich, every single day."
- **What I worked out:** "It wasn't that I didn't have time. There was just nothing that let me eat a proper hot meal without leaving my desk."
- **What I made:** "So I made this. You plug it in, drop your container in, and twenty minutes later it's hot. That's it."
- **What changed:** "First week I had hot leftovers at my desk and honestly felt like a functioning adult again."
- **The ask:** "If you're stuck eating cold lunches at work, try this. It's the only thing that fixed it for me."

Six lines straight through is a full script. None of it sounds like an ad.

## Shoot notes

Phone, front camera, arm's length like a Story. One continuous take, no b-roll, no edits. No music, no ring light, no script in hand. The product only appears when it's lifted into the same frame in beat 4. Shoot it three times, one per hook.

## Founder talking points

Give the founder the six beats as prompts and their own answers as notes, not a script to learn. They'll forget lines; they won't forget their own story.
