# Testimonial / ordered vs got

Real customers saying what happened, in their own words. Variants: a single customer talking to camera, a quick-fire compilation, "what I ordered vs what I got", or a creator reading real reviews aloud.

**Funnel:** MOF and BOF (closing people who know you). **Length:** 15 to 45 seconds. **Who:** real customers, with permission. A creator can read real reviews, framed as reviews.

## Why it works

Warm viewers have already heard the brand's pitch. What tips them is people like them saying it worked, with the specific detail only a real user would know.

## Pick it when

- The job is building a closer and the top doubt is "does it work for people like me?".
- There are real customers willing to film (permission in writing), or a strong volume of real reviews on one theme.

## Rules (non-negotiable)

- **Only real customers and real reviews.** Never invent a testimonial, a reviewer, a quote or a result.
- Permission in writing to use a customer's face, name or words. No names on screen unless they agreed.
- Paid or gifted customers are disclosed (run as a labelled partnership ad).
- Don't edit out the negative parts to change the meaning.
- Results must be typical or clearly qualified. One amazing result presented as normal breaches consumer law.

## Beats (single customer)

| Beat | ~Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0 to 3s | Their most specific line, or the result shown |
| Before | 3 to 10s | Their situation before, in their words |
| What changed | 10 to 25s | Using it, the detail only a real user knows |
| Recommendation | 25 to 32s | Who they'd tell to get it |
| Offer and CTA | 32 to 36s | Brand, offer, site (on screen or voiceover) |

## Compilation variant

3 to 5 customers, one line each, all on the same theme (the top review theme), quick cuts between them, ending on the offer. Each clip a real customer.

## Ordered vs got

The product page image, then the real thing in their hands. Proves it's real and matches the photos. Strong for apparel, gifts, home.

## Shoot notes

Ask the customer questions rather than handing them lines: "What were you using before?", "What surprised you?", "Who would you tell?". Their answers are the script.
