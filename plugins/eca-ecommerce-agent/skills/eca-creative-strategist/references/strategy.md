# Strategy (step 4)

This is the step the member is paying for. They have data and they have an idea of their customer; what they don't have is someone who turns that into "film these three, in this order, for this reason". Make the decisions, show the evidence, keep it to one page.

Contents: 1. Name the job · 2. Testing order · 3. Build angles · 4. Score them · 5. Awareness and the hook · 6. Choose the format · 7. Who films and how long · 8. Offer framing · 9. The strategy page · 10. Good vs fluffy

## 1. Name the job for this round

Pick one main job from the account read. Say which, and the number that made you pick it.

| Job | Pick it when | What the creative has to do | Success looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Feed the top** | No working TOF ad, every ad is offer-led, or CPM and frequency both rising | Stop strangers with a felt problem or moment; product is the turn; no FAQ, no heavy offer | Lands at daily frequency under 1.15, CPM at or below the campaign's lowest |
| **Build a closer** | TOF fills the funnel but nothing converts warm traffic; BOF ads with poor CPA | Answer the top objection, show proof, make the offer and the guarantee clear | Daily frequency over 1.40, lowest CPA in the campaign |
| **Replace a weak ad at its level** | An ad does its job poorly (high CPM, bad CPA for its position, little spend earned) | Same intent and position, fix the diagnosed drop-off | More spend earned, lower CPM, better CPA at the same position |
| **Iterate a winner** | A winning body, with frequency rising or only one hook ever tested | New hooks (spoken, visual, text) on the proven body; then a new body on the winning angle | A new hook beats the original's hook rate and CPA |
| **Support a launch or a real promotion** | The member has a launch, or a promotion with a real end date | Product or offer news in the first seconds, still told like a person | Launch ads earn spend in week one |

Recommend the next test even when the current winner is strong. Every winner starts tiring the day it wins, and creative takes 1 to 2 weeks to make. A structurally different ad adds delivery rather than stealing from the winner; say it's the next rung, not a replacement.

## 2. Testing order

1. **Hooks on a fixed body.** Cheapest, fastest signal. If an angle has a working body, test 3 to 5 new openers on it before writing anything new.
2. **New bodies on a proven angle.** When several hooks on one body all lose, the body or the angle is the problem.
3. **New angles and formats.** When the account's angles are worn out or a funnel stage is empty.

A round can mix them. A typical healthy round: hook iterations on the winner, plus one or two new angles for the gap.

## 3. Build angle candidates

An angle is not a topic ("comfort") or a format ("UGC"). It's **a specific person, in a specific moment, with a specific pain or desire, and the product's mechanism as the answer**, plus the proof that makes it believable.

> Shift-work nurse, trying to sleep at 10am with sun through the blinds, every mask leaks light at the nose → Morrow's moulded nose wing blocks it → "47 of 212 reviews mention day sleeping"

Pull candidates from four piles and write 6 to 10 before choosing:
1. **The account gaps**: the missing funnel stage, the untested objection, the winning angle with one hook.
2. **Customer voice**: the top reasons to buy, the moments, the unanswered questions, the surprises.
3. **Proven competitor patterns**: the mechanisms that keep getting budget; the angle nobody runs.
4. **The brand's messaging pillars** (`brand-data.md` §5), the transformation (§2) and the proof (§8).

Also define the **real category**: what does the customer buy instead for the same money, moment and job? That alternative's weakness is often the strongest angle (the cheap version that breaks, the cleaner they can't afford, the habit they're sick of).

## 4. Score every candidate

| Test | Question | Fail looks like | 1 / 2 / 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidenced | How many real customers say it unprompted? | "I think people would like..." | hunch / some mentions / repeated in reviews or survey or ad data |
| Swap test | Put a competitor's name in the line. Still true? | "High quality, fast shipping" | anyone could say it / partly ours / only we can say it |
| Demonstrable | Can the camera prove it in one take? | "Feels amazing" with nothing to show | can't show it / can show part / clear on camera |
| Track record | Winning, untested, or dead? | A dead angle rebuilt unchanged | dead unchanged / untested / proven winner or proven pattern |

Keep the top 3 that fit the job. Show the scores on the strategy page so the member can see why.

## 5. Awareness and the hook

How much the viewer already knows decides how the ad has to open:

| Awareness | Who | Open with |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware or problem-aware | Cold, TOF | The problem or moment, in their words. Product later |
| Solution-aware | Knows the category, not us | The mechanism, the comparison with the old way |
| Product-aware | Has seen us, hasn't bought | The objection, proof, other customers |
| Most aware | Warm, BOF | The offer, the guarantee, the reminder |

A TOF ad that opens with the product, or a BOF ad that re-explains the problem, is aimed at the wrong person.

## 6. Choose the format

You choose. Ask yourself, in order:
1. **What kind of evidence carries this angle?** Something the camera can prove: demo. A lived story: yapper or founder. One big doubt: comment reply. A better way than the alternative: us vs them. Lots of real customer love: testimonial. Arrival or gifting: unboxing. Three distinct benefits: 3 reasons why.
2. **What does the job need?** Feed the top: yapper, problem-solution demo, us vs them, founder story. Close: testimonial, comment reply, demo with the offer, unboxing. Iterate: the winner's own format.
3. **What does the account already over-use?** If every live ad is a 3 reasons, the next one shouldn't be. Meta's delivery rewards ads that are structurally different, not five versions of one structure.
4. **Who can actually film it?** No willing founder, no founder story. No real customers with permission, no testimonial.

Default lean: raw yapper and problem-solution ads carry most winners in ECA member accounts right now. Use them unless the evidence argues otherwise, and never make a whole round one format.

Out of scope here: statics and carousels (eca-ad-copywriting and static ad skills cover those). Note it if the account read says a static is the right test.

## 7. Who films and how long

| Who | Best for | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Paid UGC creator | Yapper, demo, us vs them, 3 reasons | Must match who the customer would see as someone like them (situation and lifestyle, never a protected trait as a requirement). Must disclose as paid partnership |
| Real customer | Testimonial, yapper, ordered vs got | Permission in writing; only their real experience |
| Founder | Founder story, comment reply, yapper | Talking points, not lines to learn; their real story only |
| Member or staff | Demo, comment reply, unboxing | Same rules as a creator |

Length: cold yappers and founder stories 30 to 60 seconds (the story earns it); demos and us vs them 20 to 45; 3 reasons 20 to 30; comment replies and unboxings 15 to 30; cut-downs 9 to 15. Length serves retention: if a second sags, cut it.

## 8. Offer framing

Value = (dream outcome x how likely it is to work) / (time x effort). The script shows the outcome, proves it'll work for this viewer, and makes it look quick and easy, without promising delivery dates or results timelines. Social proof only when real. Scarcity only when true. Check the offer in the ad matches the landing page exactly; if it doesn't, that's an Open item, not a script choice.

## 10. Good vs fluffy

Fluffy: "Angle 1: Comfort. Showcase the amazing comfort of our premium mask to elevate the sleep experience." No person, no moment, no evidence, could be anyone's.

Good: "Angle 1: Lash extensions survive the night. Women with lash extensions who've ruined a $120 set sleeping in a regular mask. The moulded cups sit off the lashes. 31 of 212 reviews mention lashes, our best-converting review theme, and no live ad mentions it. Yapper, paid creator with real extensions, 40s."
