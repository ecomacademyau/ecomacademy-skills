# Scripting (step 6)

Contents: 1. First principles · 2. The spine · 3. Retention and timing · 4. Talking points and the sample script · 5. Proof by product type · 6. Creator spec · 7. What every script delivery includes

## 1. First principles

- **One person talking to one friend.** Short sentences, contractions, fragments. Write for the ear, not the eye.
- **Open raw and mid-thought.** No greeting, no announcement. The product is the turn in the story, never the opener.
- **Start with a real problem in the customer's words.** Lift phrases straight from reviews and survey answers.
- **Specific beats clever.** Real moments, real objects, real numbers.
- **Show, don't tell.** If the camera can prove it, prove it. "It's waterproof" is weaker than pouring water on it.
- **Truth is the brief.** Every claim traces to the brand file, reviews, data or the member. Can't source it? Cut it.
- **If a line sounds like a brand wrote it**, say it out loud the way you'd tell a mate, and keep that version.

## 2. The spine (every format adapts it)

| Beat | Job | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Stop one specific person | From `hooks.md` |
| Problem | A real pain, lived | "I've tried every [category] and they all [specific failure] by [time]." |
| Agitate | One or two details that prove it's real; the workaround; the cost | "So I'm [annoying workaround] every single night." |
| Solution | Product enters, **shown, not named** | "Then I found these." (holds it to the phone) |
| What makes it different | The mechanism; passes the swap test | "It's [specific difference] instead of [what everyone does]." |
| Proof | 1 to 3 points the evidence says matter, shown or sourced | "[Proof 1], and [proof 2]." (shown) |
| Result | The payoff, on camera if possible | "And now [specific outcome]." |
| Offer and CTA | One casual line: brand, offer, URL, once. Plus the guarantee, in its real terms, if the brand has one | "Oh, they're from [Brand], they've got [offer] on. [site]." |

Rules that hold across formats:
- The **reveal shows the product**, not the logo.
- The **brand name is said once, at the end**, with the offer and URL. Saying it early makes the viewer's brain file it as an ad.
- One ask. Named to one person. Like a friend's recommendation, not a sales close.
- Get the mechanism, the top reason to buy and the offer inside the first 15 to 30 seconds. Story hooks pull watchers; the body has to turn them into buyers.

## 3. Retention and timing

- Product on screen by about 8 to 12 seconds (the founder story is the one exception: product at beat 4, around 18 seconds, because the story is the hook).
- Re-hook every 5 to 8 seconds: a new visual, the product turns, someone walks in, a new detail, a change of spot.
- Read-aloud test: about 2.5 words a second. 30 seconds is about 75 words; 45 seconds about 110; 60 seconds about 150.
- If it runs long, cut the product explanation to the steps. That's almost always where the fat is.

## 4. Talking points and the sample script

Creators and founders sound real when they know the points and say them their own way, and they sound like an ad when they read lines. So every script has two layers:

1. **Talking points**: one line per beat, in plain words, with the must-say facts marked. This is what they learn.
2. **A sample script**: "one way it could sound", written in their voice with stage directions in brackets and `[their real detail]` gaps. Never presented as lines to read word for word.

The hooks are the exception: said word for word, one take per hook, because they're the variable being tested.

Mark stage directions: `(holds it front to camera, 2 seconds)`, `[on-screen: blinking. inside the mask.]`, `[their real detail: how long they've had extensions]`.

When someone other than the member films (a creator, an ambassador, a customer), every first-person line about their own life or feelings is theirs, not ours: "I've tried every greens powder", "it's the part of my morning I look forward to". Write those as gaps or mark them "(only if true for you)", including in hooks. A paid person saying an untrue experience is a misleading testimonial, whoever wrote the line.

Every `[their real detail]` gap is written as a question they can answer on the spot, with an example of the shape: `[your real detail: what did you used to tell your friends? e.g. "I'd say I forgot my swimmers"]`. People filming tomorrow shouldn't need another round of input to fill a gap, and the example shows the size of answer that works.

## 5. Proof by product type

| Type | Angles that work | Proof on camera | Main objections | Pitfalls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Personalised / gift | The reveal, made for them, used again | Detail close-up, the recipient's real reaction | Arrival in time, sizing, returns | Delivery promises; showing names without permission |
| Beauty / personal care | Easy to apply, lasts, looks natural, saves time or money | Applied in one take; wear test, same light and angle | Too hard, irritation, looks fake | Filters, exaggerated before/after, therapeutic claims |
| Food, drink, supplements | Daily ritual, taste, swap from a worse habit | Making or pouring it, first sip reaction | Taste, price per serve | Health claims without approval, implied body effects |
| Subscription / repeat | Never run out, flexibility | Reorder history (blurred), stock at home | Lock-in, cancelling | Hiding terms, "cancel anytime" unless true |
| High-ticket | Risk reversal, cost per use, the research that went in | Longer demo, build quality | Is it worth it, what if it breaks | Long demos to cold audiences, guarantees they don't offer |
| Apparel / fit | Fit confidence, comfort, compliments | Height and size stated, front, side, back, fabric in hand | Sizing, fabric, returns | "Perfect fit", materials it doesn't have |
| Home / fragrance | The moment it creates, the room, the gift | Using it, the room, someone noticing | Strength, how long it lasts | Unsourced burn times, "non-toxic" without proof |
| Baby / kids | Calmer routines, less hassle, peace of mind | The routine done with it, in one take | Safety, sizing, washing | Safety or medical claims beyond what's certified |
| Pet | The walk, the mess, the bond | The dog or cat using it, unedited | Fit, durability, will my pet accept it | Behaviour or health claims, staged reactions |

## 6. Creator spec (one per script)

| Field | Write |
|---|---|
| Archetype | Yapper, UGC demo, founder, customer, family skit, voiceover |
| Who fits | Who the customer would see as someone like them (situation and lifestyle from the personas). Never a protected trait as a requirement |
| Setting | Where it's filmed, what's in frame one |
| Wardrobe | What they wear; product worn or held |
| Tone | e.g. warm, a bit chaotic, deadpan, fired up |
| Delivery | One take or cuts; length |
| People | How many; who walks in |
| Reference | A real ad link (from the research, their winners or the Concept Board) and what to take from it |
| Named talent | Only if verified in the member's ad names or confirmed by the member |

## 7. What every script delivery includes

Per angle: angle name and funnel stage; the 3 to 5 hooks (spoken, visual, on-screen, type); talking points; the sample script with stage directions; on-screen text for the body; the shot list inside the take, then optional extras; length (with the word count); the creator spec; the scorecard (`quality.md`); any `[CONFIRM]` items; and the ad name to use when it goes live (`quality.md` §5).
