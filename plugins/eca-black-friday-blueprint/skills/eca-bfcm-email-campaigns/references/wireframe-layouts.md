# Suggested wireframe layouts by email type

Source: the member's own reference guide. Use this to give a **layout recommendation** for every email once its content is drafted (SKILL.md Stage 5) — specific enough that Claude Design (or a human designer) can build straight from it.

## Hero

- Hero image block carrying the main offer and the time frame (dates, or "ends tonight" style urgency).
- One call-to-action button, linking to the offer/collection.
- The headline must carry genuine urgency or scarcity — that's the whole job of a Hero email. If the draft doesn't have it, it's not doing its job.

## Product

- **Section 1**: a hero offer block (as above), CTA to shop the general category/offer.
- **Section 2**: 4–10 products maximum — vary the count with the topic (a tight "top picks" email might show 4-5; a "shop the sale" browse email can go up to 10). Every product/offer shown gets its own CTA, plus one final CTA at the bottom matching the hero CTA (linking to a broad category or collection, not a single SKU).

## Education

- **Section 1**: hero with the educational angle as the headline. If the offer is simple enough to state in a few words, add it as a badge on the hero rather than a separate block.
- **Section 2**: more copy-heavy — this is where the angle gets elaborated. Use imagery to carry the point wherever possible (a comparison table, a visual how-to). Prominent headlines for scannability. Keep the whole read to about 30 seconds — attention spans are short during a sale, this isn't the place for a long essay.
- Every link must go directly to the sale products it's talking about — straight to product pages, not a generic collection.

## Plain text

- 2–3 short paragraphs, no design elements.
- Personalised to first name.
- Friendly, conversational tone throughout — this is the "note from a person" format, not a polished marketing email.
- States the offer plainly, with a genuine urgency or scarcity element (not manufactured).
- One clear CTA to shop the offer.

## Footer (every email, regardless of type)

Every email — Hero, Product, Education or Plain text — still needs a standard footer below the content: social links, an unsubscribe link, and whatever else the brand normally includes (physical address, "why am I receiving this", etc.). Use the brand's own usual footer rather than inventing one — check `eca-brand-intelligence`'s brand data if installed, or ask the member what they normally use. If nothing brand-specific is known, note it as "standard footer: social links + unsubscribe" so Claude Design still knows to add one, rather than leaving it off the layout entirely.

## Applying this

When drafting each email (Stage 5), match its wireframe to its content-type bucket from the calendar (Hero/Product/Education/Plain text — see `content-ratios.md`), then write the layout recommendation as concrete section-by-section notes the way this file does, not just "use the Hero layout." Name what goes in each section for *that specific email*, and include the footer note above — don't drop it just because it's the same on every email.
